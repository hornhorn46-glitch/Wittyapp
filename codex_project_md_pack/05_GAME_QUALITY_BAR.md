# Game Quality Bar

A game is not complete just because a window opens and something moves.

## Minimum playable quality

A playable milestone must include:

- main menu
- settings or at least audio/fullscreen/resolution toggles if applicable
- readable HUD
- pause/restart flow
- win and fail states
- at least one complete level with beginning, middle, and end
- distinct feedback for player actions
- no obvious graphical artifacts during normal play
- stable frame timing for the target platform
- no crashes during repeated restart/play cycles
- basic save/progression if the game has upgrades or unlocks

## Modern-feeling game requirements

Even a small indie prototype should feel intentional:

- coherent visual theme
- consistent UI panels/buttons/fonts
- background and buttons integrated into one composition
- clear hover/pressed/disabled states
- animations or transitions where useful
- sound feedback for important actions if audio is in scope
- camera/movement that supports the fantasy
- level composition that communicates where to go and what matters

## Forbidden low-effort results

Do not finish with:

- colored rectangles as final art when asset work was requested
- two levels with the same gameplay
- menus made of default-looking plain boxes on a flat background
- visual artifacts that are obvious within the first minute
- untested enemy/player interactions
- physics/movement that contradicts the game fantasy
- a racer where the world does not turn
- a stealth game where line of sight/sound/cover do not matter
- a tower defense where all towers feel like the same DPS knob

## Content depth target

For a small milestone, prefer fewer polished systems over many fake systems.

Good small milestone:

- 1 polished level
- 3 meaningful mechanics
- 1 strong visual direction
- tested loop
- clear extension points

Bad small milestone:

- 8 shallow levels
- 20 broken mechanics
- placeholder UI
- no testing
- unclear progression
