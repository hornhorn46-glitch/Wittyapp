# Content Safety for Tense Games

Use this file for games about hostage situations, disasters, terror, war, crime, school danger, or similar sensitive topics.

The goal is tense, respectful, fictional drama — not exploitation, instruction, or glorification.

## Design boundaries

- Use fictional locations, groups, names, and events.
- Do not recreate real tragedies.
- Do not use real victim imagery, real attack footage, or real manifestos.
- Do not provide operational real-world tactics for attackers.
- Do not reward killing as the optimal fantasy if the user requested non-violent play.
- Do not make terrorists/attackers aspirational, stylish, or heroic.
- Focus on survival, de-escalation, evacuation, communication, observation, timing, and protecting civilians.
- Keep violence mostly off-screen or abstracted when possible.
- Give the player heroic agency through restraint, cleverness, and saving people.

## Safer mechanic examples

Good mechanics:

- stay calm / manage panic
- observe patrol patterns abstractly
- hide, distract, misdirect, lock/unlock routes
- help NPCs move quietly
- find safe exits
- restore power or communication
- coordinate with rescuers through limited information
- make moral tradeoffs under time pressure
- non-lethal improvised distractions

Avoid mechanics that become real-world attack instructions:

- detailed weapon handling
- bomb-making or sabotage instructions
- realistic tactical assault doctrine
- explicit school attack recreation
- extremist propaganda systems
- scoring based on casualties

## Tone rules

The tone should be:

- tense
- humane
- serious
- restrained
- hopeful through player action

The tone should not be:

- edgy for shock value
- meme-like
- revenge fantasy centered on cruelty
- based on real victims

## Rating target

For a 16+ target, keep the experience psychological and suspenseful, with limited implied violence, no graphic gore, and no real-world instructional detail.
